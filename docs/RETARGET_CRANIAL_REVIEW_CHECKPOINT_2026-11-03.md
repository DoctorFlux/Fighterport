## Cause and correction

The exact Sonic `AttackS3S` source has a branching neck. Its old automatic `head` candidate was an animated quill branch. The native source model instead has a large directly neck-skinned central region. This checkpoint adds a **reviewed neck-local cranial surface** without inventing a head bone or mapping Sonic's neck twice. Mario's real head remains at its own bind-local transform and inherits the transferred neck motion. Unmapped quill tracks remain explicit losses rather than driving Mario's head.

The native adapter proposes direct-neck skin only after validating finite bind matrices, all represented weights, a bounded vertex count, exact skin associations and competing child-branch regions. The UI shows the measured regions and exact rig/skin SHA-256, requires the user to inspect the evidence and explicitly select the single-joint policy, then rechecks that digest during capture and independent model snapshot recapture. Changed skin or neck identity rejects the review. The review is session-only; it does not silently rewrite a saved project map.

The format-neutral solver accepts a virtual cranial point only when a native adapter supplies reviewed evidence. `head` remains absent from source joint FK mapping. Close-hand goals use the source point and a same-definition target direct-head skin centroid and RMS radius. That target metric belongs to one immutable retarget plan, so a later transfer cannot inherit it through a reused rig. Ordinary real-head Next behavior remains on its original path. The reviewed cranial majority vertices are excluded from automatic pelvis contact classification; no head floor contact is invented. The Brawl Model Previewer, native editor, Undo, roots, target binds and skin weights are unchanged.

The native UI also offers a conservative source-only single-cranial-joint case when a physical head joint is directly under the chest and has reviewable direct skin but no separate neck. It maps that joint once as semantic neck plus a virtual head surface. Deeper, ambiguous and target-side coalescing cases remain unqualified. One-versus-two shoulder chains likewise require a reviewed chain-composition contract and bidirectional tests; the existing optional shoulder mapping is not evidence that every split chain transfers correctly.
